Senior Care Costs, Priced From Public Surveys

A nursing home room costs more than most mortgages. An assisted living apartment costs more than most rents. And most families first learn either number in a hospital hallway.

National medians, 2025 survey

A family that waits until a crisis to price care pays the crisis price: whatever bed is open, at whatever rate the building quotes that week. The Genworth/CareScout Cost of Care Survey exists to replace that negotiation with a median — the middle of what facilities actually charge, surveyed across 383 cities and towns in all 50 states. The 2025 edition moved in both directions at once: residential care got more expensive, adult day care got cheaper, and home care kept its slow climb.

What the 2024 survey showed, for comparison

The 2024 edition, the baseline most planners still quote, put assisted living at $5,900 a month ($70,800 a year), a home health aide at $34 an hour, a semi-private nursing room at $9,277 a month, and a private room at $10,646 a month ($127,750 a year). Assisted living rose 10% that year, the sharpest jump in the set, while home health aide rates rose just 3% — a reminder that the labor market for home care and the real-estate market for facilities do not move together, and a plan that inflates both at the same rate will misprice one of them.

The honest caveat runs the other way, too. A national median hides the spread that decides real budgets: the same 2024 survey put assisted living in Connecticut at $8,955 a month, 52% above the national median, with a private nursing room there at $16,577. Any family pricing care from a national figure alone should treat it as a starting point and pull the state and metro medians before committing.
